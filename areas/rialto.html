Rialto sits right in the path of the winds that funnel out of the Cajon Pass, between Fontana and San Bernardino. Yards here commonly have mature shade trees, fan palms, and fruit trees, and many of the requests we see are for preventive trimming and cleanup after wind events.

Wind damage and preventive trimming

Santa Ana wind events (most common September through May, according to the National Weather Service) can drop limbs onto roofs, fences, and service drops. Thinning heavy canopies, removing deadwood, and cutting back limbs over the house are the most common preventive jobs in Rialto.

Palms in Rialto yards

Tall Mexican fan palms are everywhere here. Left untrimmed, they build up a "skirt" of dead fronds that can fall in wind and burn easily. Ask for dead fronds and seed stalks to be removed. Healthy green fronds should mostly stay, because over-pruning stresses palms.

Rialto FAQ

Should I trim my palms before the Santa Ana winds?

Many owners have dead fronds removed before fall, since that's when Santa Ana winds pick up. Check for nesting birds first if you trim in spring or summer.

How much does tree removal cost in Rialto?

It depends on the tree's size, species, condition, how close it is to buildings or power lines, access, and whether you want haul-away and stump grinding. Comparing two or three written quotes from Rialto-area providers is the best way to know. See our tree removal cost guide.
