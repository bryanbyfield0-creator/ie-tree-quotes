How much does tree removal cost in the Inland Empire?

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

We don't publish price ranges, because we haven't found a reliable, current source for Inland Empire tree removal prices, and online averages are often national numbers that don't fit local jobs. What we can tell you is what drives the price, so you can describe your job clearly and compare quotes fairly.

The biggest cost factors

  • Size and species. Taller trees and wider canopies mean more cutting, rigging, and debris. Dense hardwoods and big eucalyptus are heavier to handle than small ornamentals.
  • What's underneath. A tree in an open yard can sometimes be felled in one piece. A tree over a roof, pool, fence, or power line has to come down in small, roped sections, which takes more time, crew, and equipment.
  • Access. Can a bucket truck or chipper get close? Narrow side yards, slopes (common in Grand Terrace, Loma Linda, and other hillside areas), and backyard-only access add labor.
  • Condition. Dead, cracked, or storm-damaged trees can be less predictable and more dangerous to climb.
  • Power lines. Work near energized lines requires utility coordination and specially trained crews. Contact your utility first.
  • Haul-away and cleanup. Some quotes include chipping and hauling everything, others leave the wood. Get it in writing.
  • Stump grinding. Usually priced separately. Ask how deep they grind and whether the grindings are hauled away.
  • Permits. Street trees, heritage trees, and Joshua trees can require permits, which add time and sometimes fees. See our permit guide.
  • Urgency. Emergency calls after wind events often cost more than scheduled work.

How to get comparable quotes

  1. Send the same photos and description to each provider: the whole tree, the base, and what's around it.
  2. Ask each quote to list haul-away, stump grinding, cleanup, and permit handling.
  3. Confirm the company's CSLB license (C-49 Tree and Palm for tree work) and insurance. California requires a license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials.
  4. Be cautious of very low cash-only bids, especially after storms.

Common questions

Is stump grinding included in tree removal?

Often not. Many companies price it separately, so ask for it as a line item.

Do I need a licensed tree service in California?

For jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials (or any job that needs a permit or uses helpers), California requires a CSLB license. Tree work falls under the C-49 Tree and Palm classification.
