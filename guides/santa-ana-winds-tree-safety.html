Santa Ana winds and your trees: how to prepare and what to do after

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

The National Weather Service describes Santa Ana winds as dry, gusty winds pushed through Southern California's mountain passes by high pressure over the desert, most common from September through May. Fontana, Rialto, Rancho Cucamonga, Upland, and other cities near the Cajon Pass and the foothills often see the worst of it.

Warning signs of a risky tree

  • Dead or hanging branches ("widowmakers")
  • Cracks, splits, or a weak V-shaped fork in the trunk
  • A new lean, or soil heaving at the base
  • Mushrooms, cavities, or decay at the base or roots
  • Heavy limbs over the roof, driveway, or power lines
  • Palms with thick skirts of dead fronds

Before wind season

  • Have deadwood removed and dense canopies thinned (not topped).
  • Have dead palm fronds and seed stalks removed.
  • Have trees near structures inspected, especially large eucalyptus and pines.

After a limb or tree falls

  1. Stay away from downed power lines and anything touching them. Call 911 and your utility.
  2. Take photos for insurance before cleanup.
  3. Don't climb or run a chainsaw on a tree under tension. Hire a professional.
  4. Be wary of door-to-door crews demanding cash up front after storms, and check licenses at cslb.ca.gov.

Common questions

When is Santa Ana wind season?

The National Weather Service says Santa Ana winds are most common from September through May.

Should I top my tree to protect it from wind?

No. Topping causes weak, fast regrowth that breaks more easily. Thinning and deadwood removal are the standard approach.
