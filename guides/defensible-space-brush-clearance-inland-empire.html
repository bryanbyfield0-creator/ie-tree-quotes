Defensible space and brush clearance: what Inland Empire homeowners need to know

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Much of the Inland Empire meets the foothills and open land, and the region has a long fire history, including the 2003 Old and Grand Prix fires in the San Bernardino and Rancho Cucamonga foothills. Here's what the rules say and how tree work fits in.

The state rule

California Public Resources Code 4291 requires people who own or control buildings in state-mapped fire areas to maintain defensible space up to 100 feet from each side of a structure (but not beyond the property line). Local agencies can require more.

The zones

  • Zone 0 (0–5 feet): the ember-resistant zone right next to the house. The Board of Forestry has been developing statewide Zone 0 rules. Check the Board's page for current status.
  • Zone 1 (5–30 feet): remove dead plants, leaves, and needles, and keep trees trimmed away from the roof and chimney.
  • Zone 2 (30–100 feet): reduce fuel, space out shrubs and trees, and remove dead material.

Check your address

Use the state Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps, the San Bernardino County Fire map, or the Riverside 2025 map viewer. Your city or fire department may also send weed abatement notices with local deadlines.

Tree work that helps

  • Remove dead and dying trees near structures.
  • Limb up trees so low branches don't act as a ladder for ground fires.
  • Keep branches clear of roofs and chimneys.
  • Clean dead frond skirts off palms near the house.
  • Chip or haul debris instead of leaving piles.

Plan big clearing jobs around bird nesting season where you can. See our palm trimming guide.

Common questions

How far does defensible space have to go?

Up to 100 feet from each side of a structure, but not past your property line, under PRC 4291. Local rules can require more.

How do I know if I'm in a fire hazard zone?

Look up your address on the state Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps or the county map viewers linked above.
