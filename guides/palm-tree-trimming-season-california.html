When is palm tree trimming season in Southern California?

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Palms are everywhere in the Inland Empire: tall Mexican fan palms (Washingtonia robusta), California fan palms (Washingtonia filifera), queen palms, and Canary Island and other date palms. There's no single legal "palm season," but timing matters for the palm, for wildlife, and for wind and fire safety.

Best timing

  • Before wind season. The National Weather Service says Santa Ana winds are most common from September through May. Removing dead fronds and heavy seed stalks before fall reduces what can fall on roofs and cars.
  • Outside the main nesting months when possible. Palms are favorite nesting spots. California law prohibits needlessly destroying active bird nests, and CDFW suggests avoiding major tree work during roughly February through August where possible, or having the palm checked for nests first.
  • Before fire season for palms near structures or open land. Dry frond skirts burn easily and can throw embers.

In practice, many Inland Empire owners schedule palm trimming in late summer or early fall (after a nest check) or in winter.

How much to cut

Remove dead and broken fronds, seed stalks, and fruit. Avoid stripping a palm down to a few fronds at the top (sometimes called a "hurricane cut"). Green fronds feed the palm, and heavy pruning can weaken it over time.

Clean tools matter for date palms

UC IPM warns that Fusarium wilt, a fatal disease of Canary Island date palms, spreads on contaminated pruning tools. It recommends cleaning and disinfecting tools between palms and using hand saws rather than chainsaws on these palms. Ask your provider how they handle it.

How often?

Many fan palms are trimmed once a year. Fast-growing palms or palms near roofs and walkways may need it more often. Ask the provider to look at how much dead growth builds up in a year.

Common questions

Is it illegal to trim palm trees in spring in California?

There's no blanket ban, but it's illegal to needlessly destroy an active bird nest. If you trim during nesting season, have the palm checked first.

Why do palm trimmers clean their saws?

To avoid spreading diseases like Fusarium wilt, which UC IPM says moves between Canary Island date palms on contaminated tools.
